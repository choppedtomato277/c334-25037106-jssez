JS-SEZ Playbook, in brief

A manufacturing foothold across the Causeway

The Johor–Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ) spans over 3,500 km². It offers nine zones for investments across 11 sectors. Singapore brings its strengths as a global hub for technology, business and finance. Johor brings an abundance of land, manpower and other resources.

This page summarises the SMF JS-SEZ Playbook for manufacturers. Use it to plan your move, then ask the advisor below.

Read the SETUP framework

The nine zones

Each zone targets its own economic activity. Companies are free to set up in the zone that best suits their needs.

  • Senai-Skudai

    Aerospace manufacturing and maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO)

  • Kulai-Sedenak

    AI, quantum technology supply chain, medical devices, pharmaceuticals

  • Tanjung Pelepas

    Logistics, port operations

  • Johor Bahru City Centre

    Global services, digital economy

  • Iskandar Puteri

    Manufacturing, tourism, education

  • Forest City Special Financial Zone

    Global services, smart logistics, relocation (services)

  • Tanjung Langsat-Kong Kong

    Downstream specialty chemicals

  • Pengerang Integrated Petroleum Complex

    Oil & gas, petrochemicals

  • Desaru-Penawar

    Integrated tourism

The SETUP framework

The playbook’s SETUP framework guides companies through the essential stages of expansion.

  1. Select your zone strategically

    Zones are designed to foster innovation and serve high-growth sectors. Pick the location whose infrastructure and targeted activity fit your business model.

  2. Evaluate incentives and requirements

    Check MIDA’s JS-SEZ tax incentive packages. The Invest Malaysia Facilitation Centre-Johor (IMFC-J) and IRDA offer further incentives and facilitation support. On the Singapore side, EnterpriseSG provides financial support schemes.

  3. Take the right legal steps

    Choose a business structure: a Sdn. Bhd. (private limited company), an LLP, or a regional or representative office. Register with Malaysia’s Companies Commission (SSM) and obtain licences from the local council. Manufacturers with shareholders’ funds of RM2.5 million and above, or 75 or more full-time employees, need a manufacturing licence approved by MITI and submitted to MIDA. Register for tax and employee contributions (EPF, SOCSO, HRDF), and ensure customs compliance with the Royal Malaysian Customs Department (JKDM).

  4. Utilise bilateral support

    Singapore established a joint JS-SEZ Project Office in April 2025, bringing together MTI, EDB and EnterpriseSG. Malaysia’s IMFC-J, operational since February 2025, is a one-stop shop for setting up in the JS-SEZ. Reach out to SMF and the Federation of Malaysian Manufacturers for market entry, regulatory and partnership facilitation.

  5. Prepare your talent and supply chain

    Use the improved immigration policies for skilled worker mobility. Access Malaysia’s engineering and technical workforce, with training support from EnterpriseSG, the Johor Talent Development Council (JDTC) and MIDA. Build local supply chain partnerships, supported by MIDA initiatives.

Incentives and tax watch-outs

Incentives

  • 5% corporate tax for 10 years, or 15 years for companies with capital investment (excluding land) of at least RM500 million and RM1 billion in the manufacturing sector, for qualifying activities.
  • 5% personal income tax for knowledge workers.
  • Sector-specific incentives, such as the Global Services Hub for regional headquarters and investment allowances for smart logistics infrastructure.
  • Grants and facilitation support from both sides. For example, EnterpriseSG’s Market Readiness Assistance Grant offers up to 50% support (capped at S$100,000 per company per new market) for eligible overseas market costs.

Tax watch-outs

  • Unintended permanent establishment. Frequent work in Johor, such as supervising operations, negotiating deals or making decisions, can create a taxable presence in Malaysia. Keep clear boundaries between activities in Singapore and Johor, and keep good records.
  • Transfer pricing. Related companies in Singapore and Johor must price their dealings at arm’s length, with proper documentation in both countries. Larger companies may consider advance pricing arrangements.
  • Rules of origin and trade compliance. Inaccurate origin declarations can cost you preferential tariffs and bring penalties. Track HS codes, origin data and clearance times across both jurisdictions.
  • Cross-border commuting. The JS-SEZ does not override domestic tax rules. Malaysia may tax income attributable to workdays spent in Johor. The double taxation agreement protects you only if its conditions are met, so track workdays and keep documents.

Passes for staff

  • MIDA expatriate posts and Employment Pass

    MIDA approves two types of expatriate post: the Key Post, a permanent high-level managerial post, and the Term Post, a temporary post for a specific period. Employment Pass categories are based on minimum basic salary: Category I is RM10,000 and above, Category II is RM5,000 to RM9,999, and Category III is RM3,000 to RM4,999. Revised minimum salary requirements take effect 1 June 2026. Applications go through the Xpats Gateway System, and approvals typically arrive within 5 to 15 working days once complete documents are submitted.

  • Professional Visit Pass (PVP)

    The PVP is a temporary pass for foreign professionals on specific engagements, such as training, consultancy, or project-based work including machine installation. Holders remain employed by their overseas company. It allows a stay of up to 12 months and is not extendable.

  • Investor Pass

    Introduced in 2025, the Investor Pass eases the movement of business travellers into Malaysia. It allows a stay of up to 12 months, six months at a time, with the option to extend for a further six months. It is open to three categories: new investors, investors in the pipeline, and existing investors. Applications are made online through the Xpats Gateway System.
